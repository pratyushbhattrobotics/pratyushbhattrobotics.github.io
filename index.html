Overview

Locomotion in Biology and Robotics(LIBR) Lab · Penn State

Alternating tripod gaits coordinate two groups of three legs. By comparing different phase shifts and duty cycles, this work examines how the timing of leg movements influences locomotion efficiency.

My contributions

Project overview

Built a servo-driven robotic arm that connects mechanical construction, computer vision, and joint-angle calculations to pick up objects from a work surface and move them to a designated drop location.

How it works

  1. A webcam captures the work surface.
  2. OpenCV detects the object and identifies its position in the camera image.
  3. Image coordinates are converted into a target position on the work surface.
  4. Inverse kinematics calculates the joint angles needed to reach the target.
  5. Movement commands control the arm through the Arduino, completing the pickup and transfer.

Hardware

  • Atmega processor
  • MG996R servos
  • SG90 servo
  • External regulated 5 V power supply
  • Webcam

Software

  • OpenCV
  • ROS 2 Jazzy
  • RViz
  • Gazebo
  • MoveIt 2

What I did

  • Developed a 4-DOF robotic arm.
  • Configured ROS 2 Jazzy, RViz, Gazebo, and MoveIt 2 for robot visualization, simulation, and motion planning.
  • Implemented OpenCV object detection and converted image coordinates into positions on the work surface.
  • Used inverse kinematics to calculate joint angles and execute an automated pick-and-place sequence.

Testing & results

35 successful attempts out of 40 trials.
Achieved an 87.5% pick-and-place success rate under the tested conditions.

Self-Balancing Robot

Built a two-wheeled robot that uses inertial sensing and feedback control to maintain an upright position.

Feedback control Inertial sensing Embedded systems
Click to explore

Project overview

A two-wheeled robot is unstable when standing upright. The controller uses measurements from an MPU6050 sensor to estimate its tilt and adjust the wheels so the base moves beneath the robot as it leans.

Hardware

  • Atmega processor
  • GY-521 MPU6050 sensor
  • L298N motor driver
  • Two DC geared motors and wheels
  • 7.4 V battery
  • LM2596 buck converter

Control approach

Proportional control responds to how far the robot has tilted. Derivative control responds to how quickly it is tilting, helping reduce excessive movement and oscillation.

What I did

  • Built a two-wheeled robot that maintains an upright position by moving its wheels beneath it as it tilts.
  • Combined accelerometer and gyroscope readings to estimate tilt and implemented PD control in C++ to adjust motor speed and direction.
  • Calibrated the sensors and tuned the balancing response, accounting for the minimum motor command needed to move and adding automatic shutdown if the robot tilts too far.

Interactive Acoustic Chambers Exhibit

Project overview

Developing an interactive exhibit for Discovery Space of Central Pennsylvania that allows children to hear how different materials affect sound. The exhibit uses three chambers with different interior surfaces and an identical bell inside each chamber.

How it works

  1. Each chamber contains an identical bell connected to a pull rope.
  2. A child pulls the rope to strike the bell inside the chamber.
  3. The interior material reflects or absorbs part of the sound.
  4. The child compares the sound produced by all three chambers.
  5. The differences introduce the concepts of sound reflection and absorption.

Design

  • Three enclosed acoustic chambers
  • Identical bell inside each chamber
  • Different sound-reflecting and sound-absorbing materials
  • Pull-rope mechanism for striking each bell
  • Durable structure designed for repeated use by children

What we are doing

  • Helped develop and evaluate the initial exhibit concepts.
  • Contributed to the mechanical design of the three acoustic chambers.
  • Compared possible chamber materials, bell types, and mounting methods.
  • Considered safety, durability, maintenance, sound quality, and ease of interaction.
  • Helped develop the design based on feedback from Discovery Space.

Current development

The team is testing different interior materials to confirm that each chamber produces a noticeable difference in sound. The final design will balance educational value, durability, ease of maintenance, and acceptable noise levels.
